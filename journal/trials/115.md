# 115 · Which interrupt wins when the timer and COM1 both need service?

**The modeled master PIC accepted timer IRQ0 (`30h`) before UART IRQ4 (`34h`).** Both arrived while masked; changing the mask lost neither request. A specific end-of-interrupt (EOI) cleared only the addressed in-service bit. A UART line that stayed high after its EOI did not produce another interrupt. This is host controller acceptance, not delivery to a guest CPU.

The [fixture](https://github.com/kuro1337WStuff/hv/blob/7a04628e6f39e97dae080230f2685830b85e3f07/firmware/pit_uart_pic.h) drives the existing virtual PIT and 16450 through their port interfaces. One initialized master owns the interrupt mask (IMR), pending requests (IRR) and in-service bits (ISR). In the trace, bit `01` is IRQ0 and `10` is IRQ4. Expectations use normal fixed priority and source-specific EOIs, as in the [8259A specification](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/10wi-cs140/pintos/specs/8259A.pdf) and [Linux's legacy PIC driver](https://github.com/gregkh/linux/blob/v6.18.60/arch/x86/kernel/i8259.c).

| Observation | IMR / IRR / ISR (hex) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Both masked at pulse 16, then unmasked | `ff/11/00` → `ee/11/00` | OUT0 was low at both sampled endpoints, but rose on pulses 5, 9 and 13. The rises latched one pending bit, not three queued deliveries. |
| First acceptance; EOI0 followed by second acceptance | `ee/10/01` (`30h`); `ee/00/10` (`34h`) | IRQ4 could not interrupt IRQ0. Both vectors came from the same controller. |
| EOI4 with UART line still high | `ee/00/00` | No new request until a genuine low-to-high transition. |
| Separate trial arm: UART rearmed, then its cause was cleared | `ef/10/10`, UART line low | The UART read cleared the cause, not the edge already latched by the PIC. |
| IRQ0 nested inside IRQ4; another timer edge arrived before EOI0 | `ee/10/11` at first nested acceptance; `ee/11/10` after EOI0 | Lower-priority service survived. An IRQ cannot preempt itself. |
| Both sources masked before retiring service, then unmasked and drained | `ff/11/00` → `ee/00/00` | EOIs removed service, not pending requests. Final accepted order was again `30h`, `34h`. |
