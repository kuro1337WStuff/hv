# 122 · Can the shared UART record more than 16 bytes without losing IRQ4?

**Yes in the host model.** The ordinary fixture still accepted exactly 16 bytes. With caller-owned storage, the same UART and PIC captured 20 binary bytes (including `00h` and `FFh`) through modeled COM1 transmit writes. Two streams shared their first 19 bytes and ended in `52h` and `53h`. The capture took the UART's accepted byte, not the proposed operand.

At capacity, a ready transmitter refused `91h` without changing the platform, caller operand, transcript or guard bytes. A roomier reference with the same first 20 bytes accepted `91h` at index 20. Busy writes, divisor-latch accesses and reads did not append. Reading the UART interrupt-identification register cleared its transmit-empty cause but left the already latched PIC edge intact; a specific EOI allowed a fresh `34h` grant. The test observed 20 busy drains. Virtual S5 refused later output both with no room and with two slots free. Zero-capacity storage refused the first ready transmit; a missing buffer could not silently select the internal fixture.

MSVC also passed the previous clock, shutdown and shared-PIC controls.
